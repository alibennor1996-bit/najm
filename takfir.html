دعاوى التكفير — تفكيك بنيوي

Takfīr claims — a structural analysis

خمس دعاوى يُكفَّر بها المسلمون بعضهم بعضًا، مفكَّكة إلى مقدماتها، مع بيان موضع انكسار كل واحدة منها، وما هو موثَّق من خلاف أهل العلم فيها — بالنقل والعزو، لا بالترجيح.

Five claims by which Muslims declare one another unbelievers, broken down into their premises, with the point at which each argument fails, and what is documented of scholarly disagreement on it — by attribution, not adjudication.

How to read this page

هذه الصفحة لا تُفتي. لا تقول أيّ الأقوال صواب، ولا تُصدر حكمًا شرعيًا، ولا تنصّب نفسها مرجعًا. وظيفتها اثنتان فقط: أن تُظهر بنية الحجّة التي يُكفَّر بها، وأن تُثبت وجود الخلاف فيها بالعزو إلى مصادره.

ولهذا الثاني أثره: التكفير في جميع الحالات أدناه يقوم على دعوى أنّ المسألة قطعية لا تحتمل قولين. فمتى ثبت أن أئمة معتبَرين اختلفوا فيها، سقط أساس الدعوى — دون أن يحتاج القارئ إلى ترجيح أحدهم على الآخر.

وكل نقل هنا موسوم بحاله: ثابت النسبة والمصدر محرَّران · مختلف فيه إسناده أو تفسيره محل نزاع حقيقي، وقد بُيّن · يحتاج تحرير المصدر القول مشهور لكن لم نُحرّر نصّه من مصدر مسمّى بعد.

This page issues no rulings. It does not say which position is correct, does not pronounce on religious law, and claims no scholarly authority. It does two things only: it exhibits the structure of the argument used to excommunicate, and it establishes that disagreement exists, with attribution.

The second matters. In every case below, the takfīr rests on the claim that the question admits of only one answer. Once it is shown that recognised authorities differed, the claim’s foundation falls away — without the reader having to prefer one authority over another.

Every citation is marked: attested attribution and source verified · contested its chain or interpretation is genuinely disputed, and the dispute is stated · source needs verifying the position is widely reported but we have not yet verified a named text.

الانتقال من حكم الجنس إلى تكفير الشخص

«من قال كذا فهو كافر — وفلانٌ قالها، إذن فلانٌ كافر.»

بنية الحجّة
  1. من صدر منه القول الفلاني فهو كافر (حكم على الجنس).
  2. فلانٌ صدر منه هذا القول.
  3. إذن: فلانٌ كافر (حكم على المعيَّن).

Moving from a ruling on a category to takfīr of a person

“Whoever says X is a disbeliever — and this person said X — therefore this person is a disbeliever.”

Structure of the argument
  1. Whoever utters statement X is a disbeliever (a ruling on the category).
  2. This person uttered X.
  3. Therefore this person is a disbeliever (a ruling on the individual).
Where it breaks

The syllogism is formally valid and fails at premise one: it is a ruling on the statement, not on the speaker. Moving from the first to the second requires an independent step — establishing that in this particular person the conditions obtained and the impediments were absent — and that step is omitted rather than demonstrated.

Patterns at work
Circular reasoningAbsolute authority
Documented disagreement

What is transmitted from the early generations and the imams by way of unrestricted statements of takfīr against whoever says such-and-such is also true — but one must distinguish between the unrestricted ruling and its application to a specific person.

Ibn TaymiyyaMajmūʿ al-Fatāwā 3/230attested

No one may declare a Muslim an unbeliever, even if he errs and is mistaken, until the proof has been established against him and the way made clear to him.

Ibn TaymiyyaMajmūʿ al-Fatāwā 12/466attested

Those who have sat with me know that I am among the people most insistent in forbidding that any specific person be charged with unbelief, sin or disobedience — unless it is known that the prophetic proof has been established against him.

Ibn TaymiyyaMajmūʿ al-Fatāwā 3/229attested

The pairing takfīr al-nawʿ / takfīr al-ʿayn is a twentieth-century formulation; Ibn Taymiyya’s own terms are al-iṭlāq wa-l-taʿyīn. What is contested today is not the authenticity of these passages — they are well attested — but how consistently he applied the rule in his own rulings on named individuals, a live debate in both Arabic and Western scholarship.

«تارك الصلاة كافر خارج من الملّة، وهذا محل إجماع.»

بنية الحجّة

  2. ظاهر النص مقدَّم، ولا مسوّغ لصرفه.

Takfīr of one who abandons prayer out of negligence

“One who abandons prayer is outside Islam, and there is consensus on this.”

Structure of the argument
  1. Certain texts appear on their face to declare such a person an unbeliever.
  2. The apparent sense governs, and there is no warrant for reading it otherwise.
  3. Therefore one who abandons prayer out of negligence is an unbeliever, and no one disagrees.
Where it breaks

Premise three — that no disagreement exists — is simply false, and it is the sole source of the claim’s force. The four schools differ on this openly and in their own canonical texts, and three of them do not pronounce takfīr. One who denies the obligation is treated as an unbeliever by all four; conflating that case with negligence is where the equivocation sits.

الاستدلال بآية المائدة على تكفير الحاكم

«﴿ومن لم يحكم بما أنزل الله فأولئك هم الكافرون﴾ — والنص عام، فمن لم يحكم فهو كافر.»

بنية الحجّة
  1. الآية تُطلق وصف الكفر على من لم يحكم بما أنزل الله.
  2. لفظ «الكافرون» في الآية على معناه الأغلظ المخرج من الملّة.
  3. إذن: كل من لم يحكم بما أنزل الله كافر خارج من الملّة.
موضع الانكسار

المقدمة الثانية هي محل النزاع كله، وهي مأخوذة في الدعوى كأنها مسلَّمة. ولفظ الكفر في الاستعمال الشرعي يقع على درجات، وقد فُسّرت هذه الآية بعينها منذ الطبقة الأولى بأنها ليست الكفر الناقل عن الملّة. والدعوى تُمرِّر الخلاف في المقدمة الثانية بوصفه غير موجود.

Reading Qurʾān 5:44 as takfīr of a ruler

“‘Whoever does not judge by what God revealed — those are the disbelievers.’ The wording is general; whoever does not so judge is an unbeliever.”

Structure of the argument
  1. The verse applies the description of kufr to one who does not judge by what God revealed.
  2. “Disbelievers” here carries its gravest sense, removing one from the religion.
  3. Therefore anyone who does not so judge is outside Islam.
Where it breaks

Premise two is the whole point of contention, and the claim helps itself to it as though conceded. Kufr in scriptural usage admits of degrees, and this verse in particular has been read since the earliest layer of exegesis as not denoting the kufr that removes one from the religion. The argument passes off a contested premise as an uncontested one.

Patterns at work
Decontextualised citationManufactured consensus
Documented disagreement

“A kufr less than kufr.” In another wording: “It is not the unbelief they suppose; it is not an unbelief that removes one from the religion.”

Ibn ʿAbbās (report)al-Mustadrak 2/342; Ṭabarī, Tafsīr 10/355 (no. 12053); Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Tafsīr 4/1143 (no. 6435)contested

⚠ The chain of this report is genuinely contested and that should not be concealed. Al-Ḥākim graded it sound and al-Dhahabī concurred; al-Albānī declared it authentic (al-Silsila al-Ṣaḥīḥa 6/113, no. 2552). Contemporary fatwa bodies state plainly that its chains are disputed, and monographs have been written attacking the isnād. The point here is not to prefer one verdict — that is not this page’s business — but that the existence of the dispute defeats the claim that the matter admits only one reading.

إسقاط موانع التكفير

«الحكم ظاهر، ولا عذر بجهل ولا تأويل — فمن وقع فيه وقع عليه الحكم.»

بنية الحجّة
  1. الفعل الفلاني كفر في نفسه.
  2. لا يُعذر أحد بجهل ولا تأويل ولا إكراه في مثله.
  3. إذن: كل من فعله كافر.
موضع الانكسار

المقدمة الثانية تُلغي بجرّة قلم منظومة الموانع المتداولة في كتب أهل العلم — الجهل والتأويل والإكراه والخطأ والتقليد — وتفعل ذلك بلا برهان، بل بمجرّد التأكيد على أن «الحكم ظاهر». وظهور الحكم عند القائل ليس دليلًا على بلوغه المحكوم عليه، وهذان أمران مختلفان يُخلط بينهما عمدًا في هذه الصيغة.

الأنماط العاملة
الاستدلال الدائريإذابة الفرد في الجماعة
الخلاف الموثّق

أما الوصية: فأن تكفّ لسانك عن أهل القبلة ما أمكنك، ما داموا قائلين: لا إله إلا الله محمد رسول الله، غير مناقضين لها.

الغزاليفيصل التفرقة بين الإسلام والزندقة، ص٨٨ثابت

مهما حصل تردد، فالوقف فيه عن التكفير أولى.

محمد بن عبد الوهابالرسائل الشخصية، الرسالة الثالثةمختلف فيه

تنبيهان في محلّهما: الأول أن رسم «شروط وموانع التكفير» بهذه الصيغة المنظومة تنظيمٌ حديث جُمع من مواد كلاسيكية متفرقة، لا بابًا مستقلًا في المصنفات القديمة — وأدق العبارات أن يقال «منظومة صيغت حديثًا من مواد كلاسيكية». والثاني أن نصوص ابن عبد الوهاب المقيِّدة للتكفير ثابتة النسبة إليه، لكن أرقام صفحاتها مضطربة بين الطبعات، وقراءتها محل نزاع حادّ بين اتجاهات متقابلة — ومن الأمانة عرضها بوصفها نصوصًا متنازَعًا على تفسيرها لا حسمًا للمسألة.

Dissolving the impediments to takfīr

“The ruling is obvious; ignorance and interpretation excuse no one — so whoever falls into it falls under it.”

Structure of the argument
  1. The act in question is unbelief in itself.
  2. No one is excused by ignorance, interpretation or coercion in a matter like this.
  3. Therefore everyone who does it is an unbeliever.
Where it breaks

Premise two erases at a stroke the impediments recognised across the juristic literature — ignorance, interpretation, coercion, error, and uncritical following — and does so without argument, merely by asserting that the ruling is “obvious.” That a matter is obvious to the one ruling is not evidence that it reached the person ruled upon; the formulation trades on conflating the two.

Patterns at work
Circular reasoningDissolution of the individual into the group
Documented disagreement

As for the counsel: restrain your tongue from the people of the qibla so far as you are able, so long as they say ‘There is no god but God, Muḥammad is the Messenger of God’ and do not contradict it.

al-GhazālīFayṣal al-Tafriqa, p. 88attested

Wherever hesitation arises, withholding from takfīr is the better course.

al-GhazālīFayṣal al-Tafriqa, p. 90attested

As for what my opponents report of me — that I pronounce takfīr on suspicion, or by association, or that I declare an ignorant person an unbeliever before the proof has been established against him — that is a grave calumny.

Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Wahhābal-Rasāʾil al-Shakhṣiyya, third lettercontested

Two cautions. First, the tidy rubric of “conditions and impediments of takfīr” is a modern systematisation assembled from scattered classical materials, not a standing chapter in the classical works; “a framework systematised in modern scholarship from classical sources” is the accurate description. Second, Ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb’s restrictive letters are securely attributed to him, but their page references vary across printings and their interpretation is fiercely contested — they are presented here as texts under dispute, not as a settlement of the question.

«هذه المسألة محل إجماع، ومن خالف الإجماع فقد خرج.»

بنية الحجّة
  1. المسألة مُجمع عليها.
  2. مخالف الإجماع خارج.
  3. إذن: المخالف خارج.

The claim of consensus as a closing device

“This matter is settled by consensus, and whoever departs from consensus has left the fold.”

Structure of the argument
  1. The matter is subject to consensus.
  2. Whoever departs from consensus leaves the fold.
  3. Therefore the dissenter has left the fold.
Where it breaks

This is less a separate case than the engine driving all the preceding ones. Its power lies in converting disagreement into departure: once consensus is asserted, any further discussion becomes evidence of the discussant’s deviance rather than an objection to the claim. Testing it requires no religious training, only one question — where is the consensus recorded, who reported it, and who dissented? Cases two and three show how the answer collapses.

Any man who says to his brother ‘O disbeliever’ — it returns upon one of the two of them.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6104Book of Mannersattested

Any person who says to his brother ‘O disbeliever’ — it returns upon one of the two of them: if it is as he said, and otherwise it returns upon him.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 60bBook of Faithattested

The popular circulating form of this ḥadīth is the chapter heading used in the commentary literature, not the wording of the two Ṣaḥīḥs; the Ṣaḥīḥ wordings are given here as they stand. That precision is itself part of the argument: whoever transmits a text in its own words is more trustworthy than whoever paraphrases it and then builds a ruling on the paraphrase.
